How it is made

Raised, seated, dried

Nothing here is cast or printed. The relief goes on wet, by finger, and once it sets it stays set.

01

The base

Board or terracotta, sealed and given a flat ground coat so the relief has something of its own to grip.

02

Drawing in clay

The motif is rolled out thin between the palms and laid down line by line. Thickness decides everything.

03

Setting mirrors

Hand-cut mirror pressed in while the clay still gives, each one seated by eye so the surface never glints all at once.

04

Colour and seal

Days of drying in shade, then paint if the piece asks for it — many don't — and a matte seal against humidity.

About

One table, one plate, and hours

This work started at a folding table at home and hasn't really left it. What began with a single painted thali has become a full range — mirror mandalas, Diwali rangoli sets, shubh–labh hangings, mirror-worked matkis — but the method has not changed.

Every ring of relief is drawn by hand. Every mirror is cut and seated individually. That is why no two pieces are identical, and why a large mandala can take the better part of a week.

A mirror set perfectly straight is a mistake. The piece should change as you walk past it.
